# Alternate Rewards & Creating Collections (Ch. 5, pg. 248–249) — Reference
Source: printed pg.248–249. Die-size values confirmed by directly reading the page images (the icon-based table cells don't survive text extraction) — high confidence on Sidekick and the Temporary Ability table, moderate confidence on two Contact Die Pool rows (flagged below).

## Why this exists

Hero Points normally convert to bonuses at issue end. Some heroes' powers don't suit stacking bonuses well, or the story calls for something else — this section gives the GM three alternate ways to let a hero spend accumulated Hero Points between issues.

## Sidekick

Buy a minion with Hero Points. It acts on its own at the start of your turn. If it survives a scene, it recovers one die size automatically. If it's knocked out entirely, it can't be revived for the rest of the issue.

| Sidekick Die Size | Cost |
|---|---|
| d4 | 2 Hero Points |
| d6 | 3 Hero Points |
| d8 | 4 Hero Points |
| d10 | 5 Hero Points |

## Favor from a Contact

A contact you've established (or are calling in an old favor from) — a person, organization, or something stranger — grants a free Attack, Defend, or Overcome basic action (using the Mid die) as a reaction. More Hero Points spent = a better dice pool for the contact's action.

| Cost | Contact's Dice Pool |
|---|---|
| 1 Hero Point | d4, d4, d4 |
| 2 Hero Points | d4, d6, d4 *(worth double-checking against the physical page — this row and the one below were the least legible of the set)* |
| 3 Hero Points | d6, d8, d10 |
| 4 Hero Points | d8, d10, d12 *(same caveat as above)* |
| 5 Hero Points | d12, d12, d12 |

## Temporary Ability or Improvement

Lasts the whole issue, or until the source is removed. The same ability/enhancement shouldn't be bought again for consecutive issues.

| Benefit | Cost |
|---|---|
| Gain a temporary Red ability (needs the prerequisite power/quality) | 2 Hero Points |
| Temporarily upgrade a Red ability to Yellow | 4 Hero Points |
| Temporarily upgrade a Yellow ability to Green | 3 Hero Points |
| Increase a d6 power/quality to d8 | 2 Hero Points |
| Increase a d8 power/quality to d10 | 3 Hero Points |
| Increase a d10 power/quality to d12 | 4 Hero Points |

## Creating Collections (the GM-side mechanic)

Not to be confused with the hero-sheet Collection (6 Back Issues bundled — see the Playing the Game reference). This is GM-side guidance for planning a **story arc** spanning multiple issues.

- Brainstorm a list of possible issues within the arc, but same rule as within a single issue: don't force a single path. Build in multiple directions up front so an unexpected turn doesn't leave you scrambling.
- Anticipate plot elements you know your players want (the villain's volcano lair, the cybernetic T-rex fight) and plan those as set pieces, even if the road there is player-directed.
- You can also play the odds — anticipate what heroes will *probably* do and prep for that, the same way published adventures do — but stay flexible, since you can never be fully sure.

**Wrapping up a Collection:** the final issue of an arc is a natural point to offer heroes changes (minor through major — see Hero Advancement, pages 142–143), even outside the normal rules, as long as it fits the story:
- A dying mentor bequeaths a hero a weapon → let them add a Signature Weaponry power on the spot; they can later use normal advancement rules to make it permanent if they want.
- A hero purges an intrusive presence (e.g. a Principle of Whispers voice) → set them up to swap that Principle for a new one reflecting their changed life.
- The party discovers a villain's weakness (e.g. silver) → grant a temporary situational rule (Attacks with the right weapon Hinder with the Min die) for the rest of that arc; a hero can choose to adopt it permanently afterward, renaming abilities/equipment to match.
