# Index & Glossary — Complete (A–Z)
Source: printed pg.434–438. Read directly from the source PDF — this chapter's text extracted cleanly (no icon-based cells), so this is a complete, verified listing, not a partial one.

Page citations are preserved from the source (they often reference multiple pages where a term appears, not just its primary definition).

## A
**Abilities** — pg.12, 15, 19, 22, 44–45, 154. Tricks, tactics, or talents used in scenes by heroes and villains. For heroes, abilities fall into Green, Yellow, Red, or Out zones. Villains have access to all their abilities, regardless of zone.
**Action** — pg.12, 17, 18–23, 24–29. A thing a character does on their turn. An ability that takes an action uses the whole turn.
**Action Order** — pg.17–23, 164–165. The elective initiative system used in SCRPG — also the term for the order everyone acts in, determined one turn at a time over a round.
**Action Scenes** — pg.9, 15, 159, 161, 184. Combat or action-oriented scenes: brawls, chases, daring rescues.
**Adventure Issue** — pg.146, 252, 253–266, 267–289. Published SCRPG playable issues, like the two in Chapter 6.
**Alternate Rewards** — pg.248–249. Optional rewards replacing standard hero point bonuses, GM's choice to offer.
**Archetype** — pg.46, 72–99. Hero creation characteristic: how you use your powers and your role on a team. Determines some powers/qualities, some abilities, and your second principle.
**Attack** — pg.19, 24. An action used to deal damage.
**Auxiliary Sheet** — pg.12, 45, 443. Secondary sheet for heroes with more complicated builds.

## B
**Back Issues** — pg.10, 142. Named/numbered record of each finished issue, added to the hero sheet.
**Background** — pg.46, 49–54. Hero creation characteristic: where your hero came from before becoming a hero. Determines some qualities and your first principle.
**Basic Actions** — pg.19, 29, 154. The six fundamental action types: Attack, Overcome, Boost, Hinder, Defend, and Recover.
**Bonus** — pg.26–27. A positive mod increasing an effect die's value. Goes away after one use unless persistent.
**Boost** — pg.19, 26–27. An action to help yourself or another character, resulting in a bonus.
**Bystanders** — pg.148. NPCs who are neither foes nor notable allies — background people, often in danger from villains.

## C
**Challenges** — pg.25, 160–164, 189–199. Obstacles/dangers/complications resolved mostly via Overcome. Six types: Simple (161), Linear (162), Multiple Solutions (162), Branching Outcomes (163), Timed (164), Doomsday Devices (164).
**Collection** — pg.10, 32, 142, 249. Six Back Issues bundled and recorded once earned; invoked once per issue to maximize a die, negate a twist, or add to the story.
**Constructed Method** — pg.42. Hero creation method: skip the dice rolling, choose whatever fits your concept in each category.

## D
**Damage** — pg.19, 24. Dealt via Attack; reduces Health or is rolled against with a damage save, depending on target type.
**Damage Save** — pg.17. When a single-die character (minion/lieutenant) is attacked, it rolls its die as a save; results vary by character type.
**Defend** — pg.19, 28. An action to reduce the next damage to yourself or another character.
**Dice** — pg.17. SCRPG uses d4, d6, d8, d10, and d12.
**Dice Pool** — pg.20. The three dice rolled for an action: one from Powers, one from Qualities, one from Status.
**Distance** — pg.168–169. Not tracked as precise tactical range — addressed narratively based on what the GM and players establish.

## E
**Effect Die** — pg.21. The die applied to an action/ability from a rolled pool. Defaults to the Mid die unless an ability specifies otherwise; some abilities use multiple effect dice.
**Environment** — pg.16, 23, 157–158, 160, 240–247, 418–431. The setting of a scene. When present, gets a turn in the action order; checking the scene tracker happens on that turn.
**Example of Play** — pg.5, 18–29 (scattered), 151, 155, 158, 162–163, 167–168, 173–175, 180, 189–191, 200–204, 245. In-book illustrations of rules in action.
**Exclusive Mods** — pg.26. Mods that can't stack with another of the same type — max one exclusive bonus and one exclusive penalty per roll.

## G
**Game Moderator (GM)** — pg.8, 145–181. The player who frames the story, controls NPCs, and ensures fair/fun rules application.
**Green Zone** — pg.12–13, 147. The highest GYRO band. Green abilities are accessible any time you're not Out.
**Guided Method** — pg.42, 46. The primary hero creation method — dice rolls guide you step to step.
**GYRO** — pg.14, 16, 147. Green/Yellow/Red/Out — both the four status zones and the name of the game's core system.

## H
**Ⓗ icon** — pg.186. Represents the number of heroes in a scene; fixed for the scene even if a hero goes Out or leaves.
**Health** — pg.14, 113, 239. Measures a hero's or villain's physical health, composure, and fatigue.
**Health Range** — pg.12–13, 113, 239. The Health ranges indicating a character's current personal zone.
**Hero** — pg.2–3, 8, 41–143, 179, 181, 292–345. The main characters played by non-GM players.
**Hero Advancement** — pg.142–143. How heroes grow and change across issues.
**Hero Creation** — pg.41–141. The full process of building a hero (all of Chapter 3).
**Hero Points** — pg.14, 31. Earned via playing to Principles and roleplay; max 5 per issue; spent at the start of the next issue as bonuses.
**Hero Point Bonus** — pg.31. Floating bonuses gained by spending last issue's Hero Points, usable during the issue they're gained.
**Hero Sheet** — pg.10–13, 292–345, 439. The full record of everything needed to run your hero.
**Hinder** — pg.19, 26–27. An action to make things harder for a character, resulting in a penalty.
**Hit the Deck!** — pg.29. A basic Defend reaction to protect yourself, at the cost of a minor twist.

## I
**Index Cards** — pg.159–160. A recommended GM play aid.
**Inherent** — pg.12, 45. An ability that's always on, no activation needed.
**Issue** — pg.8–9, 142. A single play session, usually 2–4 hours.

## L
**Last Stand** — pg.17, 149. When a minion is at d4, its save behavior changes: it no longer degrades further — succeed and it survives at d4, fail and it's removed.
**Lieutenants** — pg.17, 149, 156, 160, 207, 402–417. Powerful single-die NPCs, hardier saves than minions, plus a massive-damage rule (pg.149).
**Location** — pg.16, 158, 160. A specific place within an environment with its own thematic complexity.

## M
**Major Twists** — pg.25, 29–30, 155. Twists creating notable/long-lasting problems or major threats.
**Max Die** — pg.21. The highest-rolled die in a pool.
**Mid Die** — pg.21. The middle-rolled die in a pool.
**Min Die** — pg.21. The lowest-rolled die in a pool.
**Minions** — pg.17, 148–149, 156, 160, 166, 204–206, 402–417. Standard single-die NPCs, most effective in groups.
**Minor Twists** — pg.25, 29–30, 154. Twists creating unexpected wrinkles or minor threats.
**Mods** — pg.22, 26–27, 160. Bonuses and penalties created by Boost/Hinder.
**Montage Scenes** — pg.9, 32–33, 159, 171. Narrative-focused scenes for travel, recovery, repair, investigation.
**Movement** — pg.168–169. Moving within a location costs no action; moving between locations costs your whole turn (though a Boost/Hinder/Defend can still ride along).

## N
**NPCs** — pg.8, 148–156. All GM-controlled characters: villains, minions, lieutenants, environment threats, allies, bystanders, etc.

## O
**Out** — pg.12–14, 101–104, 147. The end of GYRO — only your Out ability is usable. If the scene tracker reaches Out, the scene ends immediately and catastrophically.
**Overcome** — pg.19, 25, 154, 169. An action to get past an obstacle.

## P
**Penalty** — pg.26–27. A negative mod reducing an effect die's value. Goes away after one use unless persistent.
**Persistent Mods** — pg.26. Mods lasting until scene end or removed by action/effect.
**Personal Status** — pg.12, 147. A character's own status (Health-based, or other factors for some villains); when it differs from scene status, use whichever is closer to Out.
**Personality** — pg.46, 100–105. Hero creation characteristic: your demeanor under pressure. Determines your Out ability, status dice, and a unique quality.
**Player Characters** — the heroes played by everyone but the GM. See Hero. *(No page citation in the source — transcribed as-is.)*
**Plot Characters** — pg.150. Named NPCs advancing the story who don't always act in action scenes.
**Powers** — pg.15, 20, 47, 115–118. Talents (innate or gear-based), rated d6–d12, for both heroes and villains.
**Power Source** — pg.46, 56–71. Hero creation characteristic: what changed you into a hero. Determines some powers/qualities and some abilities.
**Principles** — pg.14, 45, 122–141. Core beliefs/personality traits. Grant a roleplay prompt, minor/major twist questions, and a Green ability that generates Hero Points. Five categories: Esoteric (124–126), Expertise (127–130), Ideals (131–134), Identity (135–137), Responsibility (138–141).

## Q
**Qualities** — pg.15, 20, 47, 119–121. Learned traits/skills, rated d6–d12, for both heroes and villains.

## R
**Reaction** — pg.12, 29, 45. Something done in response to a trigger; one per round, regained at the start of your turn (unless an ability says otherwise).
**Recover** — pg.19, 29, 32–33, 171. Special action (via ability or Montage Scene) to regain Health.
**Red Zone** — pg.12–13, 106–111, 147. The lowest GYRO band; Red abilities need your personal or scene status to actually be in Red.
**Results** — pg.17, 21–22. Numbers rolled on dice, individually or as part of a pool; can be affected by mods.
**Retcon** — pg.112. A late hero-creation step letting you tweak specific mechanical aspects.
**Risky Action** — pg.19. Adding an extra effect to a basic action by accepting a minor twist. Basic actions only, not abilities.
**Round** — pg.15, 23. A full cycle where every scene element gets a turn.

## S
**Scene** — pg.9, 159–160. A unit of play: Action, Social, or Montage.
**Scene Difficulty** — pg.185–188. Easy/Moderate/Difficult, based on threat count and severity.
**Scene Elements** — pg.146. The pieces that combine to form a playable scene.
**Scene GYRO** — pg.16, 147. The scene's own status, tracked via the scene tracker; running out of spaces sends the scene to Out.
**Scene Tracker** — pg.15–16, 23, 147–148, 159, 188. Green/Yellow/Red spaces measuring a scene's tension and danger.
**Sentinel Comics** — pg.2, 291–431. The in-fiction comic publisher (distinct from Sentinel Comics RPG, this game's title).
**Social Scenes** — pg.9, 34–35, 159, 169–170, 184. Interpersonal, dramatic scenes among characters.
**Status** — pg.14, 16, 20. A stat driven by Health (heroes, some villains) or other factors (other villains); also affected by the scene tracker. Four zones: Green/Yellow/Red/Out.
**Status Dice** — pg.12–13, 101–105. The dice assigned per zone, chosen at character creation, rolled as part of the dice pool.

## T
**Threats** — pg.150–151. Hostile minions/lieutenants/villains introduced by the scene or environment.
**Turn** — pg.9, 15. When a character or element acts; roughly 1–3 comic panels' worth.
**Twist** — pg.14, 25, 29–30, 154–155, 166–167, 200–203, 244–245. A complication from an Overcome action. Never undoes the success that triggered it.

## V
**Villain** — pg.16, 149, 152–155, 160, 179, 208–239, 346–401. The primary antagonists — name, powers, qualities, a status-determining factor, abilities, sometimes upgrades/masteries.
**Villain Approaches** — pg.152, 208–219. Villain creation characteristic: how a villain takes on obstacles.
**Villain Archetypes** — pg.152, 220–234. Villain creation characteristic: what sort of villain they are.
**Villain Masteries** — pg.149, 238. Upgraded villains' extra ability: automatic success at a specific Overcome type.
**Villain Sheet** — pg.152–153, 346–401, 445. Where a villain's running mechanics live.
**Villain Upgrades** — pg.149, 235–238. Extra abilities/elements making a villain more powerful.

## X
**X-Card** — pg.176. A safety tool protecting everyone at the table.

## Y
**Yellow Zone** — pg.12–13, 147. The middle GYRO band; Yellow abilities need your personal or scene status in Yellow or Red.

## Z
**Zones** — pg.14. Abstract representation of how dire a situation is, personally or scene-wide. Four zones: Green, Yellow, Red, Out — each with different mechanical effects on status dice and ability access.
