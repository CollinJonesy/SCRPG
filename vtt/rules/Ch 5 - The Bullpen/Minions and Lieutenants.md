# Minions and Lieutenants


Minions and lieutenants are the villain's **threat mass**. They are simpler than full characters but
follow the same die logic.


## Minions
- **Trait die:** minions range across several tiers (the book's examples run from 🎲d4 up through tougher
  examples such as gunships/megalodons well above 🎲d8), so do not assume a hard 🎲d8 ceiling.
- **Save:** when a minion takes damage, it makes a save using its trait die.
  - **Success** → the die **degrades one step** (e.g. 🎲d8 → 🎲d6 → 🎲d4) and stays in the fight.
  - **Failure** → the minion is **immediately knocked out** (removed).
- **Count:** scales with **H** (number of heroes in the scene). The book's concrete examples are
  *environment* minions — e.g. "Antibody Bots (1 per 2 Heroes)" and "Nanite Swarms (X/2)" in the
  Megalopolis lab environment (p279–287) — not generic hero-scaling tokens.


## Lieutenants
- **Trait die:** a single die (often around 🎲d6, though the book describes a range and calls 🎲d6 "more
  rare" rather than the standard).
- **Degrade only on a successful save**; on a failed save they are knocked out. They last much longer than
  minions.


## Swarms (Legion only — e.g. Biomancer, not Myriad/Overlord)
The "Instability of Form" ability is specific to the **Legion** archetype (Biomancer). On a **successful**
save, a swarm minion may **split** into two smaller dice instead of degrading (a failed save degrades one
step). Myriad's own stat block has no such ability — do not apply swarm-splitting to non-Legion villains.


## Villain "Number of Minions" tables
The villain's status die is read off this table by current minion count. **Verified examples from the
rulebook** (the book provides a generic **Overlord archetype template** at p.231, structurally like the
Legion template, with blank dice for the GM to fill in — so a generic Overlord template *does* exist):


| Villain (type) | 9+ | 5–8 | 3–4 | 1–2 | 0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Biomancer** (Legion) | 🎲d4 | 🎲d6 | 🎲d8 | 🎲d10 | 🎲d12 |
| **Edgar Black / Myriad** (Overlord) | 🎲d12 | 🎲d10 | 🎲d8 | 🎲d6 | 🎲d4 |


> *Values verified against the physical book (Biomancer p283/356; Edgar Black p358). Legion villains
> invert the curve (fewer minions = scarier); Overlords do the opposite.*


## GM notes
- Don't overthink minion math — the **scene tracker** and **environment twists** drive pacing more than
  exact counts.
- Minions exist to make heroes feel powerful and to threaten the environment; lieutenants exist to
  pressure specific heroes.
