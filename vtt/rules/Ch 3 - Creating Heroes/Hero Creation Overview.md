# Hero Creation Overview


_Source: Chapter 3, "Creating Heroes" (SCRPG core rulebook, pages 41–46)_


Building a hero means walking through a sequence of charts that establish where your hero came from, how they got their powers, how they use those powers today, and how their personality has been shaped along the way. Every hero ends up with the same set of components, but two different methods exist for filling them in.


## Two Ways to Build


### The Guided Method
The default, primary method described in the chapter. At each step you roll a set of dice, and the values (and combinations of values) you roll open up options on that step's chart. It's not fully random — you're still making choices — but chance nudges your decisions along the way. This method suits a player who has a general concept (e.g., "a cosmic hero") but hasn't nailed down the specifics; the process helps flesh out the details.


### The Constructed Method
Nearly identical in structure to the guided method, except that at each chart you simply pick whichever option best fits the hero concept you already have in mind, rather than rolling to see what's available. You still have to track the die sizes you would have rolled, because those dice get assigned to powers and qualities exactly as they would under the guided method.


Players can mix methods at the same table — discuss with your GM. If some players use guided and others use constructed, the GM may allow the guided players a bit more leeway to adjust their powers and qualities afterward.


### A Third, Informal Option
For an experienced player replicating an existing character, the GM may allow simply assigning powers, qualities, and abilities directly rather than working through either formal method — but the GM must still approve the finished hero. This isn't meant to produce a hero who maxes out every die; a hero built this way that ignores the game's balance can become "completely boring," per the rulebook's own warning.


### On Balance
Some chart options yield better dice than others, and the constructed method makes it easy to cherry-pick the best ones. This is by design — a hero with fewer or smaller dice is not meant to be weaker in practice. Abilities and how you use them matter more than raw die size; low-power heroes are meant to be just as valuable to a team as flashier ones.

### Making Existing Heroes
The archetypal heroes presented elsewhere in the book (Chapter 7) were built using this exact process, so there's no special or more powerful path reserved for "official" heroes — any hero you make with these rules is on equal footing.


## Rolling Dice to Choose From Tables


At each step that calls for a die roll, you may take **the result of any single die you rolled, or the sum of any two of the dice you rolled**, to make your table selection. For example, rolling 🎲3, 🎲5, and 🎲7 lets you choose from table entries 3, 5, or 7 (single-die results), or 8, 10, or 12 (sums of any two of those three dice).


## Assigning Dice to Powers and Qualities


Separately from picking table entries, many steps also have you take the dice you just rolled and assign each one — by its **size**, not the value rolled — to a specific power or quality on your sheet. Only the die's size (e.g., 🎲d6, 🎲d8) matters for this purpose, not what number came up.


If a step tells you to assign a die to a power or quality you already have, you have two options: apply the new die to upgrade the existing entry (freeing up the old die to use elsewhere in that same step), or keep the existing die as-is and use the new die on a different option in the same step. Either way, no die goes to waste.


## Defining Abilities


Many abilities reference "[power]" or "[quality]" in their game text — a placeholder for a power or quality you select at the time you take that ability. Once chosen, that binding is permanent for the life of the hero (barring the changes allowed under [[11_Hero_Advancement]]). Some abilities also require other bracketed choices, such as which basic action type (Attack, Defend, Overcome, Boost, Hinder) or which elemental/energy type applies. Rename each ability to fit your hero's concept once you've made these choices.

## Auxiliary Sheets


Some of the more complex hero options call for an auxiliary sheet — extra space for notes, reminders about how an ability works, NPC names, doodles, etc. It's optional if you don't need the extra room.
