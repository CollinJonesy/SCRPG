# Abilities and Actions

Every action in SCRPG is one of **six types**, each with a standard icon. Abilities (the special
powers on character sheets) are built from these.


## The six action types
| Action       | Icon           | What it does                                         |
| ------------ | -------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| **Attack**   | ✊ fist         | Harm a target; deal damage / force a status drop     |
| **Defend**   | 🛡 shield      | Protect yourself or others against incoming effects  |
| **Boost**    | ⤴ up-chevron   | Improve a die / grant a bonus to yourself or an ally |
| **Hinder**   | ⤵ down-chevron | Impose a penalty die on a target                     |
| **Recover**  | ➕ plus         | Restore Health                                       |
| **Overcome** | ↻ curved arrow | Surmount an obstacle, environment, or social barrier |

## How abilities are written
An ability entry has: **Icon** (action type), **Name**, **Type** (Action/Reaction/Inherent),
and **Game Text** (what it does). The book defines exactly three ability-type letters: **A** (Action),
**R** (Reaction), **I** (Inherent — "always on" truths). There is no "Upgrade" hero-ability type;
character growth between Issues is handled by other rules, not an ability type. Many abilities are *restricted* to a status zone (Green/Yellow/Red) or to a specific trigger (e.g. "when you take damage," "when a minion is destroyed").

## Bonuses
A **bonus** is a *numeric* mod (a positive modifier) created by a Boost and applied to the effect die rolled on a later roll — it is **not** a separate die added to the pool. Some bonuses are **exclusive** (only one of their kind active) or **persistent** (last until an effect removes them, or at most until the end of the scene — not merely until your next turn).

## Reactions vs. Actions
- **Action** abilities are used on your turn.
- **Reaction** abilities fire when their trigger happens (even mid-enemy-turn).
- **Inherent** abilities are always-on truths (e.g. "automatically succeed at X").

## Combining
A single action can use **Max die** for the primary effect and **Min+Mid dice** for side effects
(Hinder a second target with Mid, penalize a third with Min, etc.). This is the engine behind big, cinematic villain turns.
