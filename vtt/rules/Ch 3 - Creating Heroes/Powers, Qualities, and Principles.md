# Powers, Qualities, and Principles


_Source: Chapter 3, "Creating Heroes" (SCRPG core rulebook), "Powers, Explained" (p115), "Qualities, Explained" (p119), and "Principles" (p123)_


## Powers, Explained


Powers are the exceptional traits inherent to a hero — the things that make them a cut above ordinary people. A power might come from a supernatural source, a genetic quirk, or dedicated training; regardless of origin, it's mechanically treated the same way. Per the "What Goes Into a Hero?" section, powers are rated from 🎲d6 (above average) up to ⚠️ Needs physical-book confirmation: highest die size for powers is described only as "godlike" — the icon is missing from the source text.


Powers are organized into categories, each containing several named powers with a short description of what having that power means for a hero:


- **Athletic** — Agility, Speed, Strength, Vitality
- **Elemental/Energy** — a set of element/energy types (e.g., Metal, Plants, Stone, Toxic, Transmutation) that a hero can command
- **Intellectual** — Awareness, Deduction, Intuition, Lightning Calculator, Presence
- **Mobility** — Flight, Leaping, Momentum, Swimming, Swinging, Teleportation, Wall-Crawling
- **Psychic** — Animal Control, Illusions, Postcognition, Precognition, Remote Viewing, Suggestion, Telekinesis, Telepathy
- **Technological** — Gadgets, Inventions, Power Suit, Robotics
- **Self Control** — Absorption, Density Control, Duplication, Elasticity, Intangibility, Invisibility, Part Detachment, Shapeshifting, Size-Changing
- **Materials** — a set of powers letting a hero manipulate specific substances


There are also several "signature" powers (Signature Vehicle, Signature Weaponry) representing a distinctive item that's practically part of the hero, and an **Invented Power** option that lets you add a power not otherwise on the list, with GM permission.


### Inventing New Powers
Before creating a brand-new power, check whether an existing power can simply be renamed or reflavored to fit. If nothing on the list works, place the new power into the most fitting existing category so the rest of hero creation still functions. Make sure the new power isn't just duplicating something already covered elsewhere in the rules (for example, there's no standalone "Toughness" power, because that concept is already covered by the Vitality power combined with damage-reduction abilities). As always, get GM sign-off first.


## Qualities, Explained


Qualities are learned traits that come from a hero's background and experience, distinct from powers: powers are what you have access to, qualities are how you use them. Per "What Goes Into a Hero?", qualities are rated from 🎲d6 (solid competency) up to ⚠️ Needs physical-book confirmation: highest die size for qualities is described only as "world class" — the icon is missing from the source text.


Quality categories:


- **Information** — Criminal Underworld Info, Deep Space Knowledge, History, Magical Lore, Medicine, Otherworldly Mythos, Science, Technology
- **Mental** — Alertness, Conviction, Creativity, Investigation, Self-Discipline
- **Social** — Banter, Imposing, Insight, Leadership, Persuasion
- **Physical** — Acrobatics, Close Combat, Finesse, Fitness, Ranged Combat, Stealth
- **Special** — not a category of premade options; instead, this is where you write one custom quality of your own devising


### The Special Quality
As part of hero creation, every hero writes one original quality — a "high concept" or "elevator pitch" line that captures something essential about the hero that isn't otherwise represented by the standard lists. This should be broad rather than narrow: the rulebook's own example contrasts a narrow "Religion" quality with a broader "Peaceful Theologian" quality that says more about the hero and has wider utility as a fallback quality in situations without an obviously better fit.


## Principles


Principles are the guiding ideas that make a hero's motivations and story concrete. Every hero takes **two** principles during creation. Each principle provides three things, recorded on the hero sheet:


- **During Roleplaying** — guidance for how the principle shapes the hero's behavior and attitude, establishing "capital-T Truths" about the character. (For example, a hero with the Principle of the Sea generally shouldn't need to roll to survive in the ocean, barring an unusual complication.)
- **Minor Twist** — an open-ended question the GM can pose when the hero suffers a minor twist, exploring a complication with limited consequences.
- **Major Twist** — an open-ended question the GM can pose when the hero suffers a major twist, exploring a story-defining complication with major consequences.
- **Green Ability** — a "Principle of ___" ability added to the Green Abilities section of the hero sheet. These almost always give a bonus or advantage to an Overcome action tied to the principle's theme, and typically award a hero point to the whole team when used.


When recording a principle's twist questions on the hero sheet, write down the *questions themselves* — don't pre-answer them. They exist for the GM to ask when a twist actually comes up in play.


### The Five Principle Categories


Principles are organized into five categories:


- **Esoteric** (pages 124–126) — principles tied to something strange, otherworldly, or destiny-driven about the hero: e.g., Destiny, Energy/Element, Exorcism, Fauna, Flora, Future, Immortality, Inner Demon, Magic, Sea, Space, Time Traveler, Undead.
- **Expertise** (pages 127–130) — principles built around a specific field of mastery or specialization: e.g., Clockwork, Gearhead, History, Indestructible, Lab, Mastery, Mentor, Powerless, Science, Speed, Stealth, Strength, Tactician, Whispers.
- **Ideals** (pages 131–134) — principles rooted in what the hero believes in and stands for, which also marks a related vulnerability: e.g., Chaos, Compassion, Defender, Dependence, Equality, Great Power, Hero, Honor, Justice, Liberty, Order, Self Preservation, Zealot.
- **Identity** (pages 135–137) — principles about who the hero is, or isn't, underneath the mask: e.g., Ambition, Amnesia, Detachment, Discovery, Loner, Nomad, Peace, Rage, the Split, Savagery, Levity, Spotless Mind.
- **Responsibility** (pages 138–141) — principles about the burden and obligations of being a hero alongside a civilian life: e.g., Business, Debtor, Detective, Double Agent, Everyman, Family, Mask, Sidekick, Team, Underworld, Veteran, Youth.


Each category groups principles with a shared thematic flavor — for instance, Esoteric principles mark a hero as having "something more than a little strange" about them, while Ideals principles reflect that a hero's greatest strength (their belief) is tied to a matching vulnerability.


A full alphabetical Principles Quick Reference chart (listing each principle's category and page number) appears at the start of the Principles section.


### Making Your Own Principles


Custom principles are mechanically simple to build, though getting the tone right takes care — work with your GM throughout. The recommended process:


1. Decide broadly what the principle represents, both mechanically and thematically, then pick which of the five categories it belongs to.
2. Write the "During Roleplaying" text: guidance for how the principle shows up in play, plus any minor in-fiction advantages it grants. Keep this balanced — not purely a benefit or purely a drawback — and general enough to fit more than one specific hero concept.
3. Write two open-ended questions, one for the minor twist and one for the major twist. Both should be vague enough to apply to many situations rather than having one "correct" answer, and should push the hero toward interesting choices. The major twist should carry higher stakes than the minor twist; it's fine for the two to be variations on the same theme.
4. Invent the Green ability: a circumstance related to the principle where the hero gets a boost on an Overcome action. Aim for a middle ground — not so narrow it almost never applies (the rulebook's example of a too-narrow ability: only helping "in a situation involving a green octopus"), and not so broad it applies to virtually every roll (its example of too broad: any situation "where fighting is occurring").


Get your GM's buy-in at each stage of this process.
