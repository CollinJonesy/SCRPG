# Archetypes


_Source: Chapter 3, "Creating Heroes" (SCRPG core rulebook, Step 3, pages 72–99)_


## What an Archetype Represents


Your archetype is how you use your powers and how you generally operate as a hero — the role you fill on a team. Most teams field a variety of archetypes so members cover different jobs in a fight and in the story.


At the end of [[06_Power_Sources|Step 2: Power Sources]] you rolled a set of dice. In Step 3 you use those same dice two ways:


- **Table selection.** Take the result of any single die, or the sum of any two of the dice, to pick a row on the Archetypes Chart below (per the standard rolling-to-choose-from-tables rule).
- **Die assignment.** The **sizes** of those same dice (not the values rolled) get assigned to the powers and qualities each archetype lists, exactly as with the power source step.


Each archetype entry then walks you through: assigning your rolled dice to its listed powers/qualities (often with one specific power or quality required), gaining a set number of Green abilities from its list, gaining one or more Yellow abilities, and choosing a **principle** of a type fixed by that archetype (e.g., Expertise, Esoteric, Ideals, Responsibility, Identity). You finish the step by rolling for personality selection, which carries into [[08_Personalities]].


Note that most archetypes do **not** involve any "mode" mechanic — that's specific to one archetype, described below.

### A Curiosity: the Speedster Row's Stray Icon
In the reconstructed source text, the Speedster row's name cell reads "SPEEDSTER 🎲d12" — with a die-size glyph merged directly into the archetype name. There is no "Dice" column anywhere in this chart (confirmed by a prior pass over the source), so this glyph has nothing to attach to. It's almost certainly an OCR artifact — likely a stray icon that bled in from a nearby ability block during text extraction — rather than real chart data. Treat the Speedster row as having no associated die size beyond whatever your own rolled dice provide.


## Default Mode — a Modular-Only Mechanic


A "Default mode" is **not** a universal feature of every archetype. It belongs specifically to archetype #20, **Modular**. Every other archetype on the chart just gives you one fixed set of powers, qualities, and abilities — no modes, no switching.


### How Modular Works
The rulebook itself flags Modular as the most complex archetype to build and recommends against making it your first hero. Its process layers on top of the normal Step 3 flow:


1. **Pick a base archetype.** You choose a second entry from the Archetypes Chart — either another option opened up by your rolled dice, or by rerolling — and that archetype becomes your "modularized" base. Assign powers and qualities as that archetype normally directs, but skip its ability grants; you gain abilities from the Modular entry instead.
2. **Top up to four powers.** If you end up with fewer than four powers from your base archetype, add extra powers of your choice until you have four.
3. **Gain Modular's own abilities.** Modular grants its own Green ("Switch"), Yellow ("Quick Switch"), and Red ("Emergency Switch") abilities, all built around changing modes — e.g., Switch lets you Boost yourself and then change modes; Emergency Switch lets you change modes reactively when hit by an Attack, at the cost of extra damage or a minor twist.
4. **Define your modes.** You end up with two Green modes (one of which must be the **Default Mode**), and you may optionally add a free powerless mode:
   - **Default Mode** simply uses the dice assignment from your chosen base archetype, with no additional abilities gained in this mode.
   - **Powerless Mode** (optional) strips you down to two powers from your default mode at reduced die sizes, for use if your hero could plausibly be cut off from their power source (e.g., a suit that grants all their powers). While powerless, you can't use any ability except those tied to your principles.
   - A further **second Green mode** is chosen from a list (Debilitator, Improvement, or Scout Mode), each built by reassigning die sizes across a handful of powers pulled from your Default Mode and each locking out certain basic action types while active.
   - Two **Yellow modes** are chosen from another list (Analysis, Bombardment, Regeneration), and one **Red mode** from a third list (Destroyer, Hunter/Killer, Shield) — each following the same pattern of reassigning dice from your Default Mode powers and restricting which basic actions you can take in that mode.
5. **Principle and personality.** Once all modes are assigned, you choose a principle from your base archetype's options (rather than a principle fixed by "Modular" itself, which is why the chart lists Modular's principle as "Varies"), then roll for personality selection as normal.


In short: every hero has one fixed loadout for their archetype except a Modular hero, who builds a Default Mode plus several situational modes and swaps between them using the Switch-family abilities.
