# Panels, Scenes, and Structure


This page covers how SCRPG organizes play into panels, scenes, issues, and collections, plus an overview of the two pages of the hero sheet. Dice-pool mechanics, the six actions, twists, and hero points are covered in detail in [[03_Taking_Action]] — this page only mentions where they exist and what they're for.


## Pieces of the Game


In SCRPG, each player takes on the role of a **hero** — making decisions for them, narrating their dialogue, and describing how they think and feel, similar to a writer working on that character in a comic book.


**Heroes vs. NPCs:** Every character other than the players' heroes — villains, allies, or bystanders — is a Non-Player Character (NPC). One player does not control a hero at all: the **Game Moderator (GM)**. Rather than focusing on a single hero, the GM frames the story, controls NPC actions, and ensures the rules are applied fairly. (Full detail on the GM's role is in Chapter 4: Moderating the Game — see [[06_Moderating_the_Game]].)


The game leans heavily on a comic-book metaphor to organize play, both because it's thematically appropriate and because it keeps everyone focused on collaboratively creating a good story.


### Panels


A hero's turn in an action scene is described as lasting roughly 1 to 3 comic-book panels of action. Describing your action like a dynamically illustrated panel (rather than a flat mechanical description) is part of the game's expected style.


### Scenes


Every issue is made up of a series of **scenes**, of three types:


| Scene Type | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Action scenes** | Brawls, chases, daring escapes — perilous or tense situations, whether or not they involve violence. |
| **Social scenes** | Dramatic and interesting interactions among characters, heroes or NPCs. |
| **Montage scenes** | The connective tissue of the story — travel, recovery, repair, training, investigation, and other "getting from one spotlight moment to the next" beats. |


Action scenes are broken into **turns**; a turn is roughly 1–3 panels of a comic book. Full step-by-step turn mechanics are covered in [[03_Taking_Action]].

In every scene, both the GM and the hero players share responsibility for telling the story. The GM crafts, depicts, and directs; hero players aren't passive — their choices and actions shape events, informed by their principles and the twists they choose to invoke.


### Issues


A single session of play, roughly 2–4 hours, is called an **issue** — like a single issue of a comic book. Over the course of an issue's several scenes, the group typically resolves one scenario (e.g., stopping a villain's plot, solving a mystery). A longer session might cover more than one issue.


After finishing an issue, the hero players and GM collaboratively pick a title for it, and each hero player records it under **Back Issues** on their hero sheet.


### Collections


Once you've played through a related series of issues, you combine them into a **Collection** — like a collected trade paperback bundling several single issues. Mechanically, you can call back to a Collection during play to gain an advantage (see below and [[03_Taking_Action]] for the mechanical benefits). Typically, once a hero has accumulated six Back Issues (the GM may adjust this number), those issues are bundled into a named Collection and cleared from the Back Issues section of the hero sheet.


## An Illustrated Guide to Your Hero: The Hero Sheet


The rules text walks through the hero sheet using the example character **Legacy** (previously "Young Legacy," daughter of the hero once known as Legacy, now called Heritage, following the OblivAeon event).


### Hero Sheet: Page One


Page one covers your character in broad strokes — identity, motivations, and history.


- **Hero Picture** — artwork of your hero.
- **Player** — your own name.
- **Hero Name** — your heroic moniker.
- **Alias** — your "mundane" identity when not adventuring.
- **Physical Attributes** — the things people notice about your hero at a glance.
- **Characteristics** — the four building blocks of the character: **Background** (where you come from), **Power Source** (how you gained your abilities), **Archetype** (what kind of hero you are), and **Personality** (exactly what it sounds like). These map to the four steps of hero creation covered in [[04_Hero_Creation_Overview]].
- **Principles** — every hero has two principles that define who they are, including how to roleplay them and what happens on a minor or major twist tied to each. Each principle also comes with a related ability.
- **Hero Points This Issue** — a tracker of ovals marked as you earn hero points (gained by invoking principles during Overcome actions, and during meaningful social scenes); a hero can earn up to five per session.
- **Hero Point Rewards** — where you record the bonuses you convert hero points into between sessions.
- **Back Issues** — the names of completed issues your hero has taken part in.
- **Collections** — once six Back Issues accumulate, they're bundled, named, and cleared into a Collection here.


### Hero Sheet: Page Two


Page two holds what you need when your hero is actively taking action.


- **Hero Name / Alias / Player** — repeated header info.
- **Powers and Qualities** — your hero's rated traits. Rolling the die tied to a power or quality is part of building your dice pool for an action (mechanics in [[03_Taking_Action]]).
- **Status Dice** — the die you roll based on your current GYRO status (Green/Yellow/Red — Out doesn't roll a status die at all, per the surrounding text).
- **Health Range** — the health thresholds marking the boundary of each GYRO zone.
- **Current Health** — your tracked current health value.
- **Abilities** — the tricks and gear that modify how your dice results apply, unlocked according to your current GYRO status.
- **Ability Types** — each ability is tagged as:
  - **A — Action:** requires using your action for the turn.
  - **R — Reaction:** triggered in response to a specific event.
  - **I — Inherent:** always active.


Full explanation of powers, qualities, abilities, GYRO status, and dice pools is covered in [[03_Taking_Action]] — this page only describes what each hero-sheet field represents.


### Auxiliary Sheets


Some heroes are mechanically complex enough (typically because they have multiple distinct forms with different abilities) to need an extra **auxiliary sheet** beyond the standard two-page hero sheet. The example given is **Muerto**, who has a Ghost Form and several Technology Possession forms, each with different available powers and qualities; his auxiliary sheet documents how switching between forms works and what changes in each one.
