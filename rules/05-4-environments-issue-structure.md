# The Bullpen: Environments & Issue Structure (SCRPG Core Rulebook, Ch. 5)
**Source pages:** 239–249

## Environments
Not every scene has an environment; use when setting should come alive with complications.

### Creating Environments (4 steps)
1. Name (establishes scope: building, city block, county, town, city, island, space sector, dimension)
2. Establish 3 evocative traits (general characteristics, not direct powers)
3. Assign dice to traits (d4–d12; higher = greater impact)
4. Create specific twists & threats

### Traits
General characteristics representing main qualities. Examples: "Automated Weaponry" vs "Gun Turret" — broader gives more options.

### Die Size → Impact
- d4 = Minimal
- d6 = Average
- d8 = Challenging
- d10 = Dangerous
- d12 = Catastrophic

Default to d8 & d8 if unsure.

### Twists & Threats
2–3 minor twists + 1 major twist per status zone (Green/Yellow/Red). Scale increases with severity:
- **Green (Stable):** Annoyances, weirdness
- **Yellow (Decaying):** Reality distortion, escalating danger
- **Red (Collapse):** Catastrophic emergence

### Twist Mechanics
Like hero/villain abilities; keyed to environment dice. Can:
- Perform basic actions: Hinder, Boost, Attack, Defend, Overcome
- Add threats (allied/hostile/neutral)
- Introduce challenges
- Advance scene tracker
- Trigger doomsday devices
- Combine effects

**Basic actions via environment:**
- **Hinder:** Penalties (passive events that make things harder)
- **Boost:** Temporary benefits/protection
- **Attack:** Passive damage (automated defense, meteors, collapsing buildings)
- **Defend:** Temporary protection
- **Overcome:** Resolve challenges (unusual; examples: natural disaster resolves civilian danger)

## Bringing Issues Together
**Typical issue:** 2–3 action scenes + connecting social/montage scenes.

### Scene Types
- **Action:** Dangerous situations requiring resolution; not always combat-focused (natural disasters, negotiations, rescues)
- **Social:** Character interaction; roleplay; info exchange
- **Montage:** Heroes recuperate; minor actions without rolls; create bonuses for next scene

### Planning Issues
**Advance planning:** Decide social scene goals, GM character info/requirements, montage pacing.

**Rule of thumb for impromptu scenes:** Add 1 challenge, minion group, villain, or minion/lieutenant group per hero in scene.

**Montage importance:** Essential between action scenes, especially hard ones.

## Creating Collections
Collections are campaign arcs made of multiple issues.

### Worked Example: Eldritch Storm Over Rook City
**Traits:** Dimensional Rifts (d8), Horrific Distortions (d10), Malevolent Chaos (d8)

**GREEN Minor Twists:**
- A Rift Opens (hinder one target w/ Mid die)
- Imp Raid (deal Min damage to all heroes in location)

**GREEN Major Twist:**
- Help! It's Got Me! (vacuum rift pulls cars; hinder 1 hero w/ Mid die; rescue challenge required)

**YELLOW Minor Twists:**
- Touch of the Visionary (offer telepathic guidance; boost hero w/ Mid die but Visionary learns a secret)
- Eldritch Bolts (deal Mid damage to one, hinder another w/ Min)

**YELLOW Major Twist:**
- Storm Portal (d8 Storm Imps added equal to Mid die)

**RED Minor Twists:**
- Exploding Imp (deal Max damage, add Mid d8 Imps)
- Not Tentacles Again! (deal Max damage to location, add Faceless Elder Tentacle)

**RED Major Twist:**
- It Is Nearly Here (hundreds of rifts open; Elder emerges; heroes must stop cultist rituals)

**Attached Threats:**
- **Storm Imps:** d8 minions; ability converts 1 damage to persistent penalty
- **Faceless Elder Tentacle:** Lieutenant; automatically hits all targets in location; reduced by die size after each attack

- Don't use more than 1 doomsday device per environment
- Golden rule still applies: borrow & reskin from published adventures
